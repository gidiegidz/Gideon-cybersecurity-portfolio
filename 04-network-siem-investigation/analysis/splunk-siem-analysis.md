# Splunk SIEM Analysis

## Objective

Investigate the Suricata alert telemetry in Splunk, quantify the behavior of the original and tuned detection rules, and compare the resulting alert volume.

## Data Source

Suricata EVE JSON telemetry was ingested into the Splunk index:

`suricata`

The preserved data included events generated by both the original Rev 1 rule and the tuned Rev 2 rule.

The investigation focused on custom Suricata signature ID:

`100001`

Signature:

`LOCAL Possible TCP SYN Port Scan`

## Alert Investigation

The custom detection alerts were separated by rule revision using:

`index=suricata event_type=alert alert.signature_id=100001 | stats count by alert.rev`

The results showed:

- Rev 1: 80 alerts
- Rev 2: 2 alerts
- Total: 82 alerts

The Rev 1 alerts came from one controlled 100-port SYN scan. The two Rev 2 alerts came from two separate controlled 100-port SYN scans performed during tuning validation.

## Rev 1 Alert Analysis

The original Rev 1 alerts were analyzed to determine how many destination ports were represented in the alert telemetry.

The following SPL search was used:

`index=suricata event_type=alert alert.signature_id=100001 alert.rev=1 | stats count as total_alerts dc(dest_port) as unique_dest_ports min(dest_port) as lowest_port max(dest_port) as highest_port`

The results showed:

- Total alerts: 80
- Unique destination ports represented in the alerts: 80
- Lowest destination port represented: 1
- Highest destination port represented: 100

The 80 unique destination ports should not be interpreted as only 80 ports being scanned. Packet analysis with tcpdump and Wireshark independently confirmed that the controlled scan sent SYN probes to all 100 target ports.

Instead, the Splunk results show that the 80 Rev 1 alert events were associated with 80 distinct destination ports after the Suricata detection condition was satisfied.

## Rule Tuning Comparison

Because the original and tuned rules were tested with a different number of controlled scans, the raw alert totals were normalized by calculating alerts per scan.

The following SPL search was used:

`index=suricata event_type=alert alert.signature_id=100001 | eval rev=tostring('alert.rev') | eval rule=case(rev="1","Rev 1 - Original", rev="2","Rev 2 - Tuned") | eval scans=case(rev="1",1, rev="2",2) | stats count as total_alerts values(scans) as scans by rule | eval alerts_per_scan=round(total_alerts/scans,2) | table rule scans total_alerts alerts_per_scan`

The results showed:

### Rev 1 - Original

- Controlled 100-port scans: 1
- Total alerts: 80
- Alerts per scan: 80

### Rev 2 - Tuned

- Controlled 100-port scans: 2
- Total alerts: 2
- Alerts per scan: 1

Comparing alerts per scan provided a more meaningful measurement than comparing the raw totals because Rev 2 was tested twice.

The original rule generated 80 alerts during one controlled 100-port SYN scan. After tuning, each tested 100-port SYN scan generated one alert.

## SIEM Findings

Splunk provided a centralized view of the Suricata detection telemetry and made it possible to quantify the behavior of both rule revisions.

The investigation showed that:

- Rev 1 successfully detected the controlled SYN scan but generated 80 alerts from one scanning event.
- The Rev 1 alerts were associated with 80 distinct destination ports, with the alerted ports ranging from port 1 to port 100.
- Rev 2 continued to detect the controlled 100-port scans while producing one alert per tested scan.
- A second Rev 2 scan performed after the five-second window generated a new alert.
- The below-threshold 10-port test did not generate an additional Rev 2 alert.

The Splunk analysis confirmed that the tuning reduced redundant alert generation while preserving the detection behavior tested in the lab.
